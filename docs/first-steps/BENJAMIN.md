# First Steps — Benjamin

**Your lane:** the Chapter 12 endgame — the parts of the endings that were written but never fully specified, plus the story calls only you can make.
**You block:** nobody. Arinze drafts his own seam lines as he goes and sends them to you in batches; you rewrite them when it suits you.
**You're blocked by:** a playable build. Luca is putting one online — that's the one thing you're waiting on, and it's an hour of his time.

Ch 12 is the right lane for you because you wrote it. The endings are all delivered as prose and converted into the game, but the *presentation* of them was never defined — and the person who knows how they're supposed to feel is you.

## 1. Play the season · half a day — as soon as the build exists

You wrote all twelve chapters. You have never seen them **paced** — messages arriving with real delays, typing indicators starting and stopping, ghost messages deleting themselves mid-read, the phone and desktop switching under you.

Things you wrote as lines on a page are now events in time. Some will land exactly as intended and some won't, and you're the only person who can tell the difference.

Play to an ending. Notes on rhythm, not bugs (bugs go to Luca).

---

## 2. Spec the PC multi-feed interface · your call — the big one

This is the largest undefined thing in the game.

All three endings are written as a **PC battlefield view**: four or five simultaneous camera feeds — Ayo cam, Murna cam, Vi drone, Loray cam, sometimes Pitch — with private channels to Kelvin and Pitch running alongside. Your script cuts between them constantly (`[FEED — Murna cam]`), and the whole emotional design depends on it. "Uniform" in particular only works if the player is watching five feeds and can't act on any of them.

Right now that interface doesn't exist. The endings play as chat and narration, which works but throws away the staging you wrote.

**What's needed from you first (writing, no tooling):** how it should look and behave.

- What's actually in a feed panel? Text descriptions, or something more visual?
- What happens when a feed goes dark — Vi's grounded drone in Command Line, Murna's cam going still in Uniform?
- How does the player's eye get pulled to the right feed at the right moment?
- What does the layout do differently across the three endings — four feeds vs five, and Root Access's "deliberately messy" framing?

**Deliver:** a spec document. Then build it if your tooling allows; Luca picks it up if not.

## 3. The three ending screens · 1–2 hours

Luca is building the frame — a title card per ending, a summary, a route back to the menu. The frame needs your words.

For each of "Command Line", "Uniform", and "Root Access": what does the player see and read at the moment the season closes? You already wrote system-line summaries at the end of each route ("You tried to keep everyone together. You did. Almost."), and those lines are strong enough to be the ending card itself — but that's your call, not ours.

**Deliver:** copy for three screens, to Luca.

## 4. The Admin voice call · 15 min

From Ch 8 the Admin signs its messages `admin` — lowercase, "like a name it's finally decided to use." The UI still renders it as `---` everywhere.

Does it stay `---` all season, or become `admin` from Ch 8? If it changes, we can do it as a reveal — the same mechanism that retroactively renamed Murna in Ch 1's thread, so every earlier `---` becomes `admin` the moment it names itself.

**Deliver:** one sentence to Luca.

## 5. Ongoing — seam lines and the minigame verdict

**The verdict:** `MINIGAME_PROPOSAL.pdf` — nine insertions, plus reasoning for the chapters deliberately left alone. You have veto. The ones worth your scrutiny: Ch 3 (a clock during the ambush), Ch 6 (Marker Watch, built from Vi's *"there's no one here to watch them for me"*), Ch 7 (the player spotting the fake Kelvin before Murna does).

**Seam lines:** Arinze drafts them in your voice as he wires each game, and sends them over in batches of two or three. You rewrite; he drops the replacements in. Neither of you waits on the other. House rule when you're rewriting: **failure never blocks the plot** — it costs quality, a trust point, or a few lines.
